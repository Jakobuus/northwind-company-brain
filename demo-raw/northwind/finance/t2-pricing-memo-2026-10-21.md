# T2 pricing memo 2 (21 Oct)

Links earlier memo [[t2-pricing-memo-2026-09-10]] for the cost build-up.

I am replacing my earlier figure: the retail price of the Model T2 is now EUR 2,690. This memo is the one to quote. The earlier memo stays on file as the cost step, and nobody should use its price in a dealer or customer conversation.

There are two ways to set a price for the T2, and they answer different questions. Cost-plus starts from what the bike costs us to make and adds a margin. Our unit cost is EUR 1,720 and a 45% markup gives EUR 2,494. It is easy to defend inside the company because every number traces back to a supplier invoice or a labour rate. Its weakness is that it says nothing about what a rider will pay. A cost-plus price can leave money on the table when the bike is worth more to the buyer, or push us above a competitor the buyer would have chosen anyway.

Value-based pricing starts from the other end. It asks what the rider gains from the 720 Wh pack and 85 Nm motor compared with the bikes they already consider, and what that gain is worth to them. For the T2 we have two inputs. Comparable 720 Wh mid-drive bikes sit in a competitor band of EUR 2,600 to 3,100. A survey of 400 riders gives a median willingness to pay of EUR 2,750. Our price of EUR 2,690 sits in the lower half of the band and just under the median, so most riders who want the bike will see it as fair value, and we do not give away the margin cost-plus would miss.

I would use both. Cost-plus sets the floor, because a price below EUR 2,494 would lose money on every bike. Value-based sets the ceiling check, because the survey tells us where the buyer stops being interested. Between those two numbers the choice is about positioning, and price anchoring matters most here. Dealers and riders see the first number they are shown as the reference point, so the dealer sheet will show the competitor band next to the T2 and place EUR 2,690 under the top of the range. That makes the price read as the sensible option rather than the expensive one, and it limits the discount requests that come back to Sales.

Decision: I will hold EUR 2,690 for the dealer briefing and review the price again once the first 60 days of dealer orders are in.
