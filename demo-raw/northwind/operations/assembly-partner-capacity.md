# Assembly partner capacity

Prepared by Tomasz Nowak, Production Planner, 11 August 2026. Based on the site walk at Werkstatt Spree in July and the shift plans they shared with us.

## Who builds what

Final assembly of Northwind bikes is done by our contract partner Werkstatt Spree in Berlin. Frames, motors and battery packs come to them from our suppliers, and they return finished bikes to our Berlin dock for the final check and the dealer dispatch.

Assembly partner Werkstatt Spree (Berlin) builds up to 60 bikes per day on two shifts; the paint shop is the bottleneck.

## Where the limit sits

The line itself has room. The frame stations and the final test bay both finish their part of the bike before the paint shop has finished its batch. When the paint shop runs a colour change, the whole line waits, and that is where we lose most of our output in a normal week.

Three things would move the limit:

- A second paint booth, which Werkstatt Spree has priced but not committed to.
- Moving the colour changes to the end of each shift, so the line does not stop in the middle of a batch.
- Keeping the paint queue at a steady size instead of sending the frames in bursts.

## What I would do first

I would ask Werkstatt Spree to plan colour changes around the weekly build schedule, because it costs nothing and gives us the most back. The second booth is a bigger decision and should go to Sofia with a clear volume case first.

Until then, Sofia and I should plan dealer deliveries around the paint shop's rhythm, not around the nominal daily figure.
